How Much Does a Coffee Shop Make? (Revenue and Break-Even, Explained)

It's the first question almost every owner and would-be owner asks, and the internet answers it with wildly different numbers. Here's an honest look at what an independent coffee shop actually makes, how to find your own break-even point, and why chasing the average is the wrong move.

Second Manager · 8 min read

Ask how much a coffee shop makes and you'll get answers ranging from "barely scraping by" to "six figures." Both are true, because the honest answer is: it depends — on your rent, your volume, your prices, and how tightly you run the place. But you can absolutely get to a real number, and it's more useful to understand your number than to chase a national average that may have nothing to do with your shop.

The ranges, roughly: annual revenue for independent shops varies enormously — often anywhere from around $100,000 to $500,000+. Most land in the 10–20% net margin band when healthy, with survival-level shops closer to 0–5%. In dollars, that can mean monthly net profit of a few thousand dollars on typical volumes. The spread is huge because the inputs are huge.

Revenue is the loud number. Break-even is the useful one.

Knowing your revenue tells you how busy you are. Knowing your break-even tells you whether you're actually going to make it. Break-even is the sales level where you've covered every cost and start keeping money — and it's the single most important number most owners have never calculated.

The math is simpler than it sounds: break-even sales ≈ your fixed costs ÷ your gross margin. If your fixed monthly costs (rent, base labor, insurance, software) are $20,000 and your gross margin is 65%, your break-even is roughly $20,000 ÷ 0.65 ≈ $30,800 a month. Every dollar past that, after variable costs, becomes profit.

What break-even looks like in practice

For many independent shops, break-even lands somewhere around $500–$1,200 in daily sales, or roughly $25,000–$40,000 a month — which usually means serving somewhere in the range of 75–250 customers a day at a $5–$8 average ticket. Urban shops with heavy rent need to be at the higher end of that; suburban shops with cheaper rent can break even lower. The exact number is entirely yours, but the shape is consistent.

The average coffee shop's revenue is trivia. Your break-even is a target you can actually manage toward.

Where the revenue comes from

In a typical shop, beverages make up about 60–70% of revenue and food the bulk of the rest. That mix matters, because not every dollar is equally profitable — and because food is often the most underused lever an owner has for both revenue and average ticket.

How long until it makes money

Most independent coffee shops take somewhere around 12 to 24 months to reach steady profitability, depending on startup costs, ramp-up, and how fast they build a base of regulars. That's normal. The mistake is expecting profit immediately, panicking, and cutting the very things (staff, quality, hours) that build the customer base.

The bottom line

How much a coffee shop makes is the wrong question to obsess over. The right ones are: what's my break-even, am I clearing it, and which lever — traffic, average ticket, or cost control — moves me furthest past it? Those you can actually answer from your own numbers, week to week. That's the difference between running a shop on hope and running it on math.

Common questions

How much does a coffee shop make per year?

Independent coffee shop revenue varies widely, often ranging from around 100,000 to 500,000 dollars a year depending on location, size, and volume. Net profit typically lands in the 10 to 20 percent range when the shop is healthy, so the owner's take is a fraction of revenue after all costs.

How do I calculate my coffee shop's break-even point?

Divide your fixed monthly costs by your gross margin percentage. For example, 20,000 dollars in fixed costs divided by a 65 percent gross margin gives a break-even of about 30,800 dollars in monthly sales. Above that level, after variable costs, you start making profit.

How much does a coffee shop need to make daily to break even?

Many independent shops need roughly 500 to 1,200 dollars in daily sales to break even, which usually means serving about 75 to 250 customers a day at a 5 to 8 dollar average ticket. Higher-rent urban locations need the higher end; suburban shops can break even lower.

How long does it take a coffee shop to become profitable?

Most independent coffee shops take around 12 to 24 months to reach steady profitability, depending on startup costs and how quickly they build a base of regulars. Expecting profit immediately, and cutting quality or staff in a panic, is a common early mistake.
